## NeurIPS assessment and RQ3 design

Bruce asked whether the project has a real shot at NeurIPS. Four literature
and venue inquiries and a run of the RQ2 sweep gave the answer recorded in
`docs/notes/2026-09-29-neurips-assessment.md`: the regret-matched ESS, its
horizon dependence, the reliability cap, and the horizon reversal were found
nowhere; the long-horizon limit, the proportional-limit formulas, and the KL
cost of mis-set trust are classical.

Results that contradict the proposal: the single-query ratio reaches 8.5,
not "up to three"; mis-set trust is cheap for a Bayes-optimal learner unless
trust is exactly 1, which weakens RQ2's explanation for base models; and a
wrong description is abandoned after 1 to 3 examples.

Bruce decided to frame the paper on precision and reliability, with
dimension as a property of the task, and to keep the network experiments as
simple as possible. The RQ3 prompt now follows Huang & Ge's prefix layout
($d + 2$ numbers per token), the description states only $m$, each model has
one precision and one reliability, and the output has two components. The
RQ2 tables were committed with a README.
